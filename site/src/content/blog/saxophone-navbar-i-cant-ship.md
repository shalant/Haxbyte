I've been staring at a navigation problem. My portfolio has the usual top-left logo, About, Experience, Skills, and a CTA — but it also has web design, services, consulting, a blog, and a dark mode toggle. That's too many items to make sense as one nav bar.

## Discovery, Claude Code style

So I did what I usually do: brought Claude Code in for discovery. I'd found a site called Navbar.Gallery — a huge catalog of nav patterns I'd never seen before. I picked a handful I liked and fed them in.

The first round came back with two things: a pile of solid ideas riffing on my current color scheme and borrowing from the navs I'd pointed at, and a clear signal that I actually preferred a different style and theme than my portfolio currently uses. I asked for more, just to keep brainstorming.

## The riskiest idea on the board

One idea jumped out: the "Radial Logo Menu," where the logo mark itself is the trigger. No nav bar at all — just a mark in the top-left corner. Hover it, and the links bloom out radially. Claude's own assessment: "the riskiest thing on this board — playful and unusual, but genuinely might read as gimmicky rather than confident. Included for contrast."

I had it generate ten more riffs on that idea. All cool, all pretty out there.

## Then a saxophone bell walked in

Somewhere in there I had the idea to make the links flow out of a saxophone bell instead of a plain logo mark. Claude didn't know what a saxophone bell was, which cracked me up. That one question kicked off a completely different round of ideas — stuff I never would have landed on writing it myself.

So I found my favorite-looking saxophone — an unlacquered classic Selmer Mark VII tenor — pasted it in, and got some genuinely wild combinations. Here's the last one: the nav links actually bloom out of the bell.

## Reality check

I also keep a career-development folder next to my portfolio work, so I asked Claude whether this idea belonged there or somewhere else. The obvious answer was my music site — so I switched repos and tried slapping it on.

That's when the rubber met the road. It didn't work on tablets or other larger touch screens at all — there's no hover to trigger it — and the design system I'd spent hours perfecting clashed with it far more than I expected going in.

## The musician's dilemma

I've faced this choice before, in my other life. Do you chase an idea purely for the creative payoff — pure invention, no practical use? The artist in me says yes, every time. That's how you build a real personal language: stealing licks from the masters, chasing an idea that shows up at 2am, turning a student's mistake into something that becomes part of your own vocabulary.

So here it is: a saxophone that blooms into a navbar. I can't use it, and I really shouldn't. But come on — isn't it cool?
