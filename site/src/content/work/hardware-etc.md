## What it needed

Hardware Etc LLC sells into multi-family residential construction — a market where the buyer is usually a developer or GC comparing suppliers before they ever place a call. The old site wasn't doing that job: a single unstyled placeholder page, default browser typography, no layout, no branding beyond a plain wordmark. Whatever credibility Hardware Etc had came from relationships, not the site.

![Hardware Etc's original site — an unstyled placeholder page with default browser typography and no layout](/work/hardwareetc-before.jpg)

## What I built

I rebuilt it on Squarespace — the right call for a small business that needs to update copy and product photos on its own, without a developer in the loop, and doesn't need a custom app just to sell hardware.

The harder problem was that this site has two real audiences who use it completely differently: spec-writers doing careful research at a desktop, and crews on a job site pulling up a spec sheet on a phone mid-install. Neither could be the "default" case — navigation, image weight, and information hierarchy all had to hold up seated at a laptop and standing in a hallway with a phone.

![Homepage hero — dark navbar and split layout over real interior photography](/work/hardwareetc-hero.jpg)

The private-label cabinet line needed to read as a real, structured product line, not an afterthought — bulk buyers comparing options at volume expect a catalog, not a single page bolted onto a site built for something else. So it got organized and photographed as its own section.

![Value proposition section — source direct, bulk purchasing, quick-ship, private label, laid over supporting photography](/work/hardwareetc-2.jpg)

Everything else — quick-ship messaging, clear service breakdowns — is aimed at a B2B buyer running a comparison, not a walk-in retail customer looking to be sold to.

![Product category grid, including the private-label cabinet line as its own catalog section](/work/hardwareetc-5.jpg)

## How it turned out

The site now serves as the business's professional front door — the first credibility check a developer or GC runs before they ever pick up the phone.
